What Is the Rice Purity Test?

I first took the Rice Purity Test during my college orientation week. I was sitting in a dorm room with my roommate, and we were both laughing at the questions and comparing scores by the end of the night. That is what this test is. It is a simple checklist quiz, not a real exam or anything official. If you have never taken it before, think of it as a casual quiz that measures life experience, not intelligence or morals.

It goes back to 1924 at Rice University in Houston, Texas. The student newspaper there made a short survey to see how "pure" someone was, based on their life experiences. The first version had only around 10 simple questions. By the 1980s, it had grown into the full 100-question version.

It was made for freshmen during Orientation Week. It was a fun icebreaker to help new students bond, instead of the usual awkward small talk.

Taking it is simple. You check the boxes next to anything you have experienced. When you finish all 100 questions, you get a score between 0 and 100. A higher score usually means fewer boxes checked, so more "innocent". A lower score means more life experiences checked off.

There is no good or bad number here. When I took it, I scored in the low 70s. By my second year it had dropped, which reflected normal college life and new experiences. Most online versions of this test do not ask for your name or email, so it is safe and anonymous to take. When you finish, your result is one number. The chart below explains what each range means in plain language.

Who Is the Rice Purity Test For?

This test is made for friends, couples and college students who want a lighthearted way to compare life experiences. Use it when you are hanging out with friends, breaking the ice with someone new, or deciding what score to share on social media.

People usually take it to compare scores with friends, start conversations about shared experiences, or retake it over time and see how their score changes. It is a fun self-assessment quiz in the tradition of the original Rice University list. It is entertainment and self-reflection, not a scientific or medical test. Your answers stay private in your own browser.

How Does the Rice Purity Test Work?

Taking this quiz online takes about five minutes, and the rules could not be simpler. Work your way through the 100 statements in the checklist above and tick every box that describes something you have done. Be honest. Nobody sees your answers, and the result is only interesting if it is true. When you reach the end, press Calculate My Score and your score appears instantly.

The scoring is plain subtraction. You start at 100 and lose one point for every box you check. A small item counts the same as a big one. Tick 8 boxes and you score 92. Tick 41 boxes and you score 59. There is no timer, no pass mark and no ranking against other people. Your answers are saved on your own device, so you can close the page and finish the test later without starting again. This version runs entirely in your browser. No sign-up, no download.

One test, one simple idea

The test above is the classic Rice Purity Test. It is the traditional 100-question list, from holding hands to the wilder stuff, all scored the same way. You start at 100 and lose one point for every box you tick. Everyone answers the same 100 questions, so scores are directly comparable with friends.

Why do people take the Rice Purity Test?

A century after it began as an orientation-week icebreaker, the test survives because it is a perfect conversation starter. Friends compare scores in group chats. Couples use it to swap stories they have never told each other. Students retake it each year to watch the number drift as university life happens to them. It works because the questions do the talking. Every checked box is a memory, and every score is a short story about a life so far. It is also completely private here. No sign-up, no email, and no posting your answers anywhere unless you choose to share your result card yourself.

Rice Purity Test FAQs

What is the Rice Purity Test?

The Rice Purity Test is a 100-question checklist that started at Rice University in Houston, Texas. You tick every life experience on the list that applies to you, and your score is 100 minus the number of boxes you checked. It is a fun icebreaker about life experience, not an exam, a medical test, or a judgement of your character.

What is a rice purity score?

A rice purity score is the number you are left with after the subtraction. It is 100 minus the boxes you checked. It is not a grade. It only says how many of the listed experiences apply to you, and nothing about your character.

How is my Rice Purity Score calculated?

Your score is simple subtraction. You start at 100 and lose one point for every statement you check. Check 12 boxes and your score is 88. Check 55 boxes and your score is 45. There is no weighting and no hidden algorithm. Every question counts exactly the same.

What is a good Rice Purity Score?

There is no good or bad score, because the test measures experience, not quality of character. As a rough guide, scores between 90 and 100 usually mean only a few of the listed experiences. Scores from 60 to 89 are where many students land. Scores below 60 reflect a wider range of experiences. You can read our full guide on what counts as a good score for a detailed, honest breakdown.

What is the average Rice Purity Score by age?

Scores usually drift down as people get older, because life adds experiences over time. In casual, self-reported comparisons, high-school students often score in the 80s and 90s. College students commonly land somewhere in the 60s to 80s. Adults in their late twenties and beyond often sit lower. These are informal patterns people report, not scientific statistics. Your own number depends on your life, not your birth year.

Is the Rice Purity Test anonymous?

Yes. The test on this page runs entirely in your browser. Your answers are not sent to a server, you do not need an account, and we never ask for your name or email. Your progress is only saved on your own device so you can come back to it, and you can clear it at any time with the Clear Checkboxes button.

Is there only one version of the test?

Yes. This site offers the one classic Rice Purity Test: the traditional 100-question list, scored from 0 to 100. There are no separate editions here, so everyone answers the same 100 questions and scores are directly comparable.

Can I retake the Rice Purity Test?

Yes, as often as you like. Many people retake the test every year or two to see how their score changes as they collect new experiences. Use the Clear Checkboxes button to start again from a blank list.

Why is my score different from my friend's score?

Because the test only reflects the specific experiences on its list. Two people the same age can have completely different scores, depending on where they grew up, what their social life looks like, and which chances came their way. A difference in score says nothing about who is better, smarter or more mature. It means you have lived different lives so far.

How long does the Rice Purity Test take?

About 5 minutes. There are 100 simple checkboxes and no time limit, so you can go through the questions at your own pace.

Can I share my score with friends?

Yes. After you calculate your score, this site creates a score card with a caption. You can download it or share it on WhatsApp, X and Facebook.

Is this the official Rice Purity Test?

The first version came from The Rice Thresher, the student newspaper at Rice University. This site offers the same classic 100-question format online, and it is not affiliated with Rice University.

Can I take the test on my phone?

Yes. The test works on phones, tablets and computers, and your score is calculated instantly in your browser on any device.

Is the test accurate?

Not in a clinical or scientific sense. It is a self-reported list made for fun and social bonding, not for measuring anything about your mind. Your answers are personal, the questions were never designed to weigh the same, and the score gives only a rough impression from a short list of experiences.

Does the test reveal personality traits?

Not directly. The test only counts experiences from its list. It does not measure personality, character or any mental trait. But thinking about your score can still teach you something about yourself, like what you value and what kind of life you want.
