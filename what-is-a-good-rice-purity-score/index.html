What Is a Good Rice Purity Score? The Honest Answer

You have your number. Now comes the question every single person asks, usually within ten seconds: is that a good score? This page gives you the honest answer — the one your friends were too busy laughing to explain — along with the ranges people tend to feel comfortable in, the ranges that raise eyebrows in a group chat, and the one situation where a score genuinely deserves a second thought.

The Honest Answer: There Is No Good or Bad Score

The Rice Purity Test does not grade you. It counts. Every box you tick is an experience from a list written by university students in the last century, and the final number simply reports how many of those experiences you have collected. A high score means few of them apply to you yet. A low score means many of them do. Neither says anything about your honesty, kindness, intelligence or future — the qualities that actually make a life good are nowhere on the list, mostly because they would make a very boring quiz.

So when people ask what a good Rice Purity Score is, what they usually mean is something narrower: what score would look normal — not shocking, not suspicious — if I shared it with my friends? That is a social question, and it does have a practical answer, which depends mostly on your age and your circle.

If you forced most students to pick one band and label it "good", they would probably point at the 70s and 80s — enough experience to have stories, not so much that the number becomes the story. But notice how much that answer depends on the audience. The exact same score of 48 can look worldly in a first-year dorm and positively tame at a thirty-year-old's dinner party. When a Score Is Worth a Second Thought

There is one exception to the "it is all just fun" rule, and it is worth stating plainly. The total number never matters — but a handful of individual items on the list describe behaviour that can genuinely hurt you or other people, such as dangerous driving, serious legal trouble or anything involving pressure and consent. If you ticked boxes like those, the useful response is not shame about a number; it is an honest look at those specific choices. Everything else on the list — the parties, the crushes, the all-nighters, the questionable haircuts — is just life, and life is the entire point of the test.

How to Actually Use Your Score

The best use of a Rice Purity Score is the use it was invented for a hundred years ago: starting conversations. Compare editions with a partner using the Couples Edition and you will hear stories no direct question would ever produce. Retake the Standard test once a year and watch which sections move — the category breakdown in your result shows exactly where your new experiences came from. And if your number is very different from a friend's, treat it as an invitation to swap stories rather than a ranking, because that is all it has ever been. Scores, Confidence and the Comparison Trap

A quiet problem with sharing scores is that the comparison rarely stops at the number. Someone with a 92 can leave a group chat feeling strangely behind, as if everyone else has lived more. Someone with a 31 can feel judged for a life they enjoyed and would happily live again. Both reactions give a student party game far more authority than it has earned. The test cannot see your friendships, your work, your family, your health, your faith, your creativity or any of the hundreds of experiences that never made it onto a list written for American college freshmen generations ago.

If you want a healthier way to hold your result, compare it only with your own past result. A score that moved from 84 to 71 over two years of university is a small, honest record of a life being lived — new places, new people, a few mistakes you learned from. That is the comparison the test was always meant for, and it is the only one where the word "good" really belongs: a good score is simply an accurate one, from a list you answered honestly, in a life you recognise as your own.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a Rice Purity Score of 70 good?
By the standards most people use when comparing with friends, yes — a score around 70 sits in the comfortable middle. It usually suggests some life experience without an especially long list. But remember the test has no pass mark: 70 is not better than 50 or worse than 90 in any meaningful sense.
Is a low Rice Purity Score bad?
No. A low score means many of the listed experiences apply to you. Some of those experiences are harmless fun, some are milestones, and a few are genuinely risky — judge individual choices on their own merits, not the total. The number itself carries no moral weight.
What is the most common Rice Purity Score?
There is no verified data, because results are anonymous. From scores people share publicly, the middle ranges — roughly the 50s to 80s for students and young adults — appear most often, while scores at the extreme ends, very close to 100 or below 20, seem comparatively rare.
Can my Rice Purity Score go back up?
No. The score counts experiences you have had, and experiences cannot be un-had, so on the same edition of the test your score can only stay the same or go down over time. That is exactly why friends enjoy retaking it every year or two and comparing the change.
