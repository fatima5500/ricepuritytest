Average Rice Purity Score by Age: Typical Ranges Explained

Almost everyone who finishes the Rice Purity Test asks the same follow-up question: is my score normal for my age? It is a fair question, because a score of 62 means something very different for a 15-year-old than it does for a 35-year-old. This guide explains the typical ranges people commonly report at each stage of life, why those ranges move the way they do, and why you should hold them lightly.

One honesty note before the table. The Rice Purity Test is an anonymous entertainment quiz, not a survey run by researchers. Nobody collects verified data from everyone who takes it, so there is no official average Rice Purity Score by age published anywhere. What exists instead are patterns: thousands of people voluntarily share their scores with friends, in group chats and in online communities, and over the years a consistent shape has emerged from those self-reported results. The ranges below summarise that shape. They are useful for context and useless as a judgement — please use them only for the first.

Why Scores Fall Fastest Between 18 and 22

Look at the table and one thing jumps out: the drop between the late teens and the early twenties is much bigger than any other step. That is not because students suddenly become wilder people. It is because several life changes arrive at the same time. Most people live away from their parents for the first time, manage their own money and schedule, meet hundreds of new people, and gain easy access to parties, travel and relationships. The original test was literally written for this moment — Rice University students created it during orientation week in the 1920s as a way for new students to compare how much of campus life they had already tasted.

After graduation the curve flattens. Through the late twenties and thirties, scores tend to drift down slowly rather than drop, because most adults have already encountered the common situations on the list and the remaining unchecked boxes are the more unusual ones. Plenty of people also retake the test years later and find their score has barely moved — a sign that the test measures a particular slice of youthful experience, not the whole of a life.

Age Is Only One Factor

Two people of exactly the same age can easily be thirty points apart, and the reasons are usually obvious once you look at their lives rather than their birthdays. Where someone grew up matters: a student in a big city simply meets more of the listed situations than someone in a small, quiet town. Culture and family expectations matter. So does personality — some people seek out every new experience on offer, others happily prefer a small circle and early nights. Money matters too, because travel and nightlife cost money. None of these differences map onto maturity, intelligence or worth, which is why comparing your score only with your own age group is still just a rough guide, not a verdict.

It also helps to remember what the list leaves out. The questions were written by and for students, so they say a lot about parties, dating and campus mischief and almost nothing about work, family, creativity, hardship or achievement. An adult who has built a business, raised children and travelled for work can score surprisingly high because the test never asks about any of that. How to Use These Averages Sensibly

Use this page the way you would use a horoscope you actually trust a little: for fun, for conversation, and for a small moment of self-reflection. If your score sits inside the typical range for your age, that tells you your experiences so far are broadly similar to those of many people at your stage of life. If it sits far outside it, that is equally fine — it means your path has been different, and different is the raw material of every good story. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average Rice Purity Score for a college student?
There is no official statistic, because the test is anonymous and informal. In casual, self-reported comparisons shared by students online, college students often land somewhere in the 60s to 80s early in their studies and drift lower by graduation. Treat that as a rough pattern, not a benchmark you are supposed to match.
Why do Rice Purity Scores fall with age?
The test is a checklist of experiences, and experiences accumulate with time. Every year adds chances to travel, work, date, celebrate and make mistakes, so the typical number of checked boxes slowly rises and the score slowly falls. It is arithmetic, not a change in character.
Can an adult have a very high Rice Purity Score?
Yes, and it is more common than people expect. The list is heavily weighted towards a particular student lifestyle. Adults whose lives took a quieter, more private or more focused path can score in the 80s or 90s at any age — and that says nothing good or bad about them.
Should I worry if my score is below the average for my age?
No. These ranges are informal patterns people report, not health data or norms. A lower score usually just means your life has included more of the specific situations on this particular list. If a question on the test points to something risky or illegal, that behaviour is worth thinking about on its own — the total number is not.
