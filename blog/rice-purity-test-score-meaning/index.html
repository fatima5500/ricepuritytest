Rice Purity Test Score Meaning: What Your Number Actually Says

When I finished the Rice Purity Test for the first time, I stared at my score for a solid minute trying to figure out if it was "good." Nobody explains the rice purity test score meaning when you take it. You just answer a hundred yes-or-no style questions, hit submit, and get thrown a random number between 0 and 100 with zero context. I remember texting my friend "is 68 bad??" and she had no clue either. That confusion is exactly why I wanted to break this down properly, using what I've learned from taking it myself, retaking it later, and comparing notes with a bunch of friends over the years.

Your score isn't a grade and it's not judging your character. It's simply a reflection of how many life experiences from that 100-question list apply to you. The fewer boxes you check, the higher your score climbs. The more boxes you check, the lower it drops. That's the entire system. No hidden math, no weird algorithm — once you understand how the rice purity score works, the number stops feeling so mysterious.

How the Score System Actually Works

The test has 100 statements, each one describing something you may or may not have done. Things range from pretty mild stuff to more personal categories like relationships, parties, and other adult experiences.

Here's how I'd explain the mechanics if you're doing this rice quiz for the first time:

Read each question carefully instead of speed-clicking through them. Some are worded in a tricky way and easy to misread if you're rushing. If the statement applies to you, check the box. If it doesn't apply, just leave it unchecked and move to the next one. Once you've gone through all 100 questions, submit the test. Your final score appears instantly, somewhere between 0 and 100.

Basically, every box you check subtracts a point from your total. So if you check 4 boxes out of 100, your score lands around 96. If you check 55 boxes, your score drops to around 45. It's a simple subtraction system dressed up as a quiz — and once you see it laid out, it's easy to understand how the rice purity test works from start to finish.

I remember my roommate checking almost nothing during orientation week and ending up with a 96. We joked about it for weeks, but honestly it just meant she hadn't had many of the specific experiences listed, nothing more dramatic than that.

Breaking Down the Score Ranges

After taking the test a couple of times and watching probably twenty other people take it too, I've noticed the results tend to fall into five general zones. None of these are official categories carved in stone, but they're a pretty accurate rice purity score chart of how most people interpret the numbers.

Score 100–91: Very Innocent This is the highest range, and it usually shows up in people who are younger or just haven't had many of the experiences the test asks about. I've mostly seen this range among incoming freshmen during their very first week of college, before they've settled into campus life. It's not a bad thing, it just reflects limited exposure so far.

Score 90–77: Relatively Pure This range still leans on the lower-experience side but usually includes small things like dating casually or breaking a minor rule here and there. A lot of college students I know landed somewhere in this bracket during their first semester.

Score 76–45: Moderately Experienced This is honestly the most common range I've seen among people in their late teens and early twenties. It reflects a fairly balanced mix, some relationship experience, some social exploration, nothing extreme in either direction. When I retook the test during my second year, I landed right in the middle of this zone.

Score 44–9: Quite Experienced People in this range have gone through a much wider variety of experiences from the list, often involving deeper relationship history or more social risk-taking. This range shows up more in older students or people who've just lived a more eventful few years.

Score 8–0: Highly Experienced This is the lowest range, meaning almost every box on the list applies. It's rare, and honestly I've only seen a handful of people land here, usually after years of a pretty active social and dating life.

What Counts as an "Average" Score

A question I get asked a lot is what does the rice purity score mean on an average day, or what a normal score even looks like. Based on what I've seen across my own friend group and various online discussions, the average tends to sit somewhere between 60 and 80 for college-aged people. That range reflects a fairly balanced amount of life experience, not too limited, not too extreme.

Some sites throw out a number close to 72 as the average among students specifically at Rice University, though this really depends on the group and the year. Scores shift depending on the crowd you're comparing against, so don't treat any single number as some universal benchmark.

Does Age Change the Score?

Yes, and this pattern is pretty predictable once you think about it. Younger people naturally score higher simply because they've had less time to check off boxes on the list. As people get older, they usually rack up more experiences, so the score trends downward. Even a rice purity test for 14 year olds tends to land much higher than one taken by someone in their twenties, simply because of the age gap in life experience.

From what I've noticed among different age groups:

Younger teens, roughly 13 to 16, tend to score somewhere in the 85 to 100 range. College freshmen and late teens, around 17 to 20, often land between 70 and 90. People in their early twenties, 21 to 25, usually fall between 50 and 75. Anyone past 25 tends to average somewhere between 40 and 70.

These aren't exact numbers, more like general trends I've picked up from comparing scores across different age groups over the years. Your actual number can swing a lot depending on your personal lifestyle, not just your age.

Is a High Score Actually "Better"?

I get asked this constantly, and my honest answer is no, not really. A high score just means you haven't experienced many of the specific things listed in the test. It doesn't reflect anything about your personality, values, or how interesting you are as a person.

A low score works the same way in reverse. It just means you've had more of those experiences, whether that's dating history, social risk-taking, or other categories covered in the questions. It says nothing about whether you made good or bad choices.

I've seen people get genuinely stressed over their number, especially in group settings where scores get shared around. From personal experience, the healthiest way to treat this test is as a lighthearted personal reflection, not a scoreboard to compete over.

Highest and Lowest Possible Scores

The scoring range is simple: 0 to 100, nothing outside that.

A score of 100 means you answered "no" to literally every question on the list. This is extremely rare once people get into their college years, mostly because at least a handful of everyday life experiences tend to apply to almost everyone by that point.

A score of 0 means the opposite, every single box checked. This is just as rare on the other end and usually only shows up after years of a very eventful lifestyle.

Most people, myself included, land comfortably somewhere in the middle, and that's completely normal.

Can You Retake the Test?

Yes, and honestly this is one of the more interesting parts of the whole thing. There's no rule against retaking it, no cooldown period, and no account tracking your previous attempts. I took it during freshman orientation and again near the end of sophomore year, purely out of curiosity, and watched my score drop by almost twenty points in that time.

It's a genuinely fun way to track how your life has shifted over a year or two, especially if you keep screenshots of your old results. Just don't expect anything scientific out of it, it's more of a personal snapshot than an actual measurement of anything.

A Few Mistakes to Avoid

After watching so many people take this test, a few common slip-ups keep showing up.

Rushing through the questions is probably the biggest one. Some statements are phrased in ways that are easy to misread if you're skimming quickly, which throws off your actual result.

Another mistake is comparing your score directly against friends without context. Age, lifestyle, and personal circumstances all affect the number, so a direct comparison rarely tells the full story.

Lastly, don't treat the score as some kind of identity label. It's a private, informal quiz, not a certified personality assessment.

Wrapping This Up

At the end of the day, your Rice Purity Test score is just a number reflecting which boxes you checked on a list built by college students almost a hundred years ago. It doesn't measure your worth, your character, or how "good" of a person you are. I've taken it a couple of times myself, laughed about the results with friends, and moved on with my day. If you're curious about your own score, take it, have a laugh, and don't overthink the number too much.

FAQs

Q. What is a good rice purity test score?
There is no officially "good" Rice Purity Test score. The test is just a fun self-assessment, so your result isn't meant to be judged.
Q. What does a score of 100 mean on the rice purity test?
A score of 100 means you answered "no" to all questions in the test. In simple terms, it shows that you haven't experienced any of the situations listed in the quiz.
Q. What does a score of 0 mean?
A score of 0 means you answered "yes" to all questions in the test. It shows that you have experienced everything listed in the quiz.
Q. Is a higher score on the rice purity test better?
No, a higher score just means less experience, not better character.
Q. What is the average rice purity test score for a 16-year-old?
For a 16-year-old, the average score is usually around 85 to 100. At this age, most people have had limited life exposure.
Q. Is 77 a good rice purity score?
There's no single "good" number, but 77 usually falls in the "relatively pure" range, meaning a fairly light amount of life experience compared to the average college student.
Q. How does the rice purity test work?
You go through 100 statements and check off any that apply to you. Each unchecked box adds a point, so fewer checked boxes means a higher, more "pure" score.
