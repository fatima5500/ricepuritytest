The History of Self-Reflection Tests

Long before social media algorithms, viral TikTok trends, or interactive web applications, students passed around printed paper questionnaires in college dorm rooms. Discover how a 1920s freshman orientation survey evolved into one of the most famous cultural benchmarks on the internet.

1. The 1924 Rice University Origins

The origins of the Rice Purity Test date back over a century to 1924 at Rice University in Houston, Texas. Created initially by female students residing in Jones College dormitories, the original test was drafted as a lighthearted survey designed to measure social experiences and bonding among incoming female freshmen.

It comprised a series of 100 checklist items covering everyday high school experiences, social etiquette, dating milestones, and minor rebellious acts of the roaring twenties.

1924

Initial paper version created at Rice University for female student orientation and icebreaking.

1980s

Spread across North American college campuses via student newspapers and mimeographed flyers.

1990s

First HTML digital versions uploaded to early university Usenet groups and web servers.

2020s

Modern interactive mobile versions with multiple specialized editions and score card downloads.

2. The Paper & Campus Newspaper Era (1950s–1980s)

Throughout the middle of the 20th century, the test remained a staple of American college culture. Passed down by student orientation committees, fraternities, and sororities, handwritten and typed mimeographed copies circulated through dormitories every autumn.

College newspapers frequently printed updated versions of the questionnaire, adapting questions to reflect changing social norms, fashion trends, and youth culture of each decade.

3. Transition to the Early Web (1990s–2000s)

With the birth of the World Wide Web in the 1990s, computer science students digitized the 100 questions into basic HTML web pages. Early internet users shared links via Usenet newsgroups, AOL Instant Messenger (AIM), and early blogging platforms like LiveJournal and Myspace.

This digital transition allowed the test to transcend its physical campus roots and gain an international audience among high school and university students worldwide.

4. Modern Virality & Multi-Edition Era

In recent years, the test has undergone a massive resurgence on social platforms like TikTok, Instagram, and Reddit, where users post their score cards and compare results with friends and romantic partners.

Today's digital platforms have expanded far beyond the original 1924 list, introducing specialized editions such as College Edition (100 Qs) for university students, Clean & Safe for younger audiences, Couples Edition for romantic partners, and Wild Adventure for thrill-seekers.
