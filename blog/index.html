Most people meet the Rice Purity Test the way students have met it for a hundred years — somebody hands it to them, usually laughing. Our featured post this season is the long read: the history of the Rice Purity Test, from a handwritten 1924 survey in a Rice University dorm, through photocopies and the campus newspaper, to the 100-question version that ended up on every student's phone. Start With the Test, Then Come Back and Read Your Score Properly

Here's the order we'd recommend, and it's the order most readers end up following anyway. First, take the Rice Purity Test — a few minutes, 100 questions, no sign-up, and your answers never leave your device. Enjoy the number for a moment. Then, when the questions start — is that normal? is that good? what do people my age usually get? — that's what this blog is for.

Work through it in whatever order your curiosity takes you. The score meaning chart decodes your exact range. The average score by age guide puts your result next to the ranges people your age typically report, with the honest caveats those numbers deserve. Our answer to what a good score actually is might be the most reassuring thing you read today. And if the mechanics are what's bugging you — how the counting works, whether your answers are saved, what the five editions are for — the FAQ page settles those in about two minutes.

New guides land here when readers ask us something we can't answer in a paragraph. If there's a question about the test you wish existed as a full article, tell us — that's genuinely how most of these pages started.
